Early Season Tremors: A preliminary perspective of the 2026 SJ Earthquakes season

Did you feel the jolt on Thursday morning? There's more to come

Originally posted on substack on April 05, 2026

After you sit in the sun for a while, maybe reading a piece of paper or a book, the world changes when you look up. Your eyes are tinted. It’s warm and orange now. It feels like a summer day, and the sun feels like a friend who's served you a languid lunch under the Santorini sun. I wouldn’t know, I’ve never been.

I finished rereading the match guide. It was still spring in San Jose. On the field below, the players were more raucous than they have ever been. They channel something otherworldly through them. You feel as though you’re part of something, you feel faith in something bigger. I’ve made it a constant point in my life to not find or recognize that in any religious way, but I constantly fail whenever I encounter it.

The first time I had felt that energy through soccer was the famed Spanish team from the Catalan capital; FC Barcelona. There were incredible individuals, names who can stand all on their own. Messi, Iniesta, Busquets, Xavi, Pique, Eto. So many more. But your faith rises from their synergies, not from them alone. How they all became actors in a cell, acting tirelessly for their host. An assembly line feels too rigid and utilitarian, it was a living breathing cell instead. And it was that which helped start my love with team sport.

This year has started off decidedly different for the Quakes. And I think we are channeling a new energy. So sound the alarm. Call the front papers. Find Krazy George and tell him to go… crazy.

After the first five games of the 2026 MLS season , the San Jose Earthquakes have four wins. Twelve points out of a possible fifteen. Four clean sheets too - not a single goal conceded three weeks in. Two wins at home, and two wins away. Sure, the fourth game, we lost to Seattle at home 1-0. But we played really well and probably should have scored a couple. The fact that fans were disappointed we didn’t beat Seattle? That’s already wild, and clearly indicative of change from last season.

I had been convinced this season was going to go horribly (and part of me still thinks this will all come to a grinding halt soon). We have an owner on the verge of selling the team (therefore does not give a single shit), four of our best players left the team (including the ex-captain), and there is still no bottled beer sold at the stadium. Only cans. Alright, maybe the other things are more relevant.

Here’s all the goal contributions from the Earthquakes’ 2025 season. The ones in red are the ones that left the team.

That’s most of our goalscorers and playmakers. Chicho, Josef Martinez, and our all-time assists leader and captain Cristian Espinoza are gone. The most productive attacking players. The loss of Espinoza really hurts, but what hurts even more is that it apparently wasn’t even something the Earthquakes wanted or intended to do. It was a complete accident. They (someone on the Quakes’ technical staff) forgot to resign his contract when it ended, and so Espinoza became a free agent. Off to Nashville he’s gone, where I want to root for him… but I can’t say it doesn’t hurt. He had a big game recently too, scoring to knock Miami and Messi out of the CONCACAF Champions League.

The question that comes to mind though, is how does a professional organization forget anything like that? Resigning the contract of their most important player. I guess that’s just some Quakes shit. We then went the majority of the offseason not making signing, just losing players. I thought the team had given up before the first game had even come.

But late in the offseason, there was hope. Vancouver signed German legend and ramdeuter Thomas Muller before their playoff run last year. A big signing like that seemed unthinkable by the train wreck that is our team. But, inexplicably. Somehow. Magically. We landed German striker and speedster Timo Werner, former Champions League winner with Chelsea. The highest profile signing San Jose may have ever made.

We went on to also sign Jonathan Gonzalez, a Santa Rosa born Mexican defensive midfielder a few days before the season started. Since the signings and the successof the first few games, the expectations and support for the season has changed for all the fans from what I can tell.

The change I’m seeing is that all the experienced guys are gone. And it’s led to a kinf od addition by subtraction. It’s now a group of young and scrappy guys, all wanting to prove themselves. Nothing to lose, and everything to gain. I saw the energy under the sun in the first couple games at home. I think Timo Werner picked up on that energy when he came too. Our guys are electric. Preston Judd and Beau Leroux and Ousenni Bouda and DeJuan Jones. These guys will knock anyone else out the way, get the ball, and bring San Jose to their feet. I want Timo Werner to be scared to lose his starting spot to Preston Judd. I know these guys will give it their all and make him work for it.

I’m not saying these guys are playing some high level tiki-taka soccer. I’m not saying that we are going to continue being at the top of the table. All I’m saying is that this team is slowly becoming into a cohesive mesh. A cohesive cell that wants to win. And what better could we ask for? Maybe this is the year we finally have a deep run in the Open Cup as well, where we play Phoenix Rising to begin.

It’s a big year in general for soccer in the Bay Area. The Earthquakes potentially being good is one thing. The World Cup is also coming to the US and Levi’s Stadium , in perhaps the most tense geopolitical situation in years. Gary Singh has written some really great and thought provoking stuff on Metro Silicon Valley about the world cup coming, that I totally recommend taking a look at. What it means for Santa Clara, for San Jose urbanism, for transit here, for soccer’s place in the US, for immigrants in our country. I just hope it all goes well.

Happy Holi, happy St Patrick’s Day, and all that other fun stuff that went by. Stay safe in between these devastatingly early heat waves. No disastrous implications about that whatsoever, I’m sure. And if there are, make sure you get to a game this season and have a canned beer before it all goes down. Maybe a German one at that.

Thank you for reading.

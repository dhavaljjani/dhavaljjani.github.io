Is Streetlight Records the place to be?!

You can find plastic, potheads, paganism, and a lot more on Bascom Avenue

Originally posted on substack on November 25, 2026

You will own nothing and be happy. It’s a bleak outlook and phrase but one you may have invariably heard or read online. People just love typing it and saying it. In fairness to these cynical online people, it’s the idea that modern corporations seem to pretty much strive to. Subscriptions and services that let you feel like you have what you want. But, you’re streaming or renting everything. Music, books, movies, TV, grocery services, hell the burrito you’re eating. The fine print clarifies that you don’t really own anything. Just recently, Sony announced that they would stop releasing physical game CDs for the PS5 going forward. I don’t even have a PS5 but the notion that you are now beholden to online digital purchases that are solely tied to your account and can be taken away at their whims is ridiculous.

I want to amend this in my life where I can. Who doesn’t love the details of video game manuals and flipping through CDs in the car? Poring through a pile of movies to figure out what to watch togerher. I want to start keeping more physically. I don’t want to rely on subscriptions. Especially for music. The joys of my life can’t be behind a monthly paywall. I have a few CDs but they’re all ripped MP3s for the most part. But, as I began idly scrolling facebook marketplace more in my free time, picking I started on the main street of internet shopping, on facebook marketplace.

One of the guys I met through marketplace was someone I went back to meet and buy things from a few times. The third time, on a dusty day by a convenience store in Willow Glen, he told me about Streetlight Records, the record shop. That’s where he picked up a ton of his music. There’s two Streetlight Records, one in downtown Santa Cruz and one here in San Jose. I’ve been to the Santa Cruz haunt before, just aimlessly jumping between aisles, unaware I could participate in having the music too. “I don’t have a record player!” But I do have a car, with a CD player. A world of possibilities awaits. And so I pick up a disc here, and a disc there on the marketplace. I pop them in my car every so often, and it’s a wonderful ritual. The sound quality is great. When someone joins me in my car, I enjoy letting them pick something to play. I clearly need to visit San Jose’s music Mecca to find all the music.

So, I decided I want to contribute to our world’s excessive consumerism under the guise of physical preservation. After all, what is anything but an excuse to go do something ?

Bascom Avenue in San Jose occupies a part of the city that seems to exist in a timeline in which tech money never did. It shows you a side of the area that you find when you talk to people, but not when you idly google San Jose and its parts online. It’s more than just Apple and Google and Adobe and the Winchester House. Stories from San Francisco and Oakland and Shanghai and Nottingham and Guadalajara and Lahore and so on and so on find themselves uttered in every corner of San Jose. They get retold and remixed in this strange new land. It’s cultural eutrophication of a nonliteral kind. Our rivers run dry most of the year.

The goal of the day was short and simple; to visit Streetlight Records. A place where the voices of the past echo but the present still speaks too. And also sings and riffs and yells and screams and everything else.

I don’t hang around here too much. Bascom Avenue, when you’re a highway junkie like me, can get glossed over on 280. It could be just another exit. When I lived in Cupertino, I’d see Cupertino. When I lived in Sunnyvale, I’d see Sunnyvale. When I lived in Alum Rock, I’d see Alum Rock. Now that I live in Mountain View, I largely see Mountain View. I have no reason to be on Bascom, other than when I actively make the time out of my day to go there. I’d like to have more excuses.

On that moody day on Bascom, I drive past a building front with a facade fitting of a glitzy Friday night matinee. Except, maybe twenty years ago. The building looks more abandoned than my first book draft. It’s the Burbank Cinema, a theater with some real history. From art house to revival house to porn. It showcased all kinds of things until it was shut down in March of 2000. The building is on the county’s register of historical places, but supporters of the building hope to preserve and renovate it as a theater by adding it to the National Register of Historic Places. Here ’s a link to find more about that.

Burbank itself is technically not part of San Jose. It’s its own CDP just like the nearby Fruitdale on the other side of the Junipero Serra Freeway, aka 280. Residents of these areas do not vote in San Jose elections. They don’t receive services like fire and police from the City of San Jose. They also don’t have certain taxes to pay. Instead, they rely on themselves and county services entirely. They have their own sewer system. The reason these CDP and urban islands exist, here and around the area, is because of annexation histories. San Jose was able to gobble up Willow Glen and Alviso and Alum Rock and others for a growing tax base, but certain neighborhoods were skipped over even in the 90s when annexation was the goal. It seems what this has led to though, is tighter bonds in these communities for neighbors. If you’re interested in reading more about Burbank, I would recommend reading this article and this article . They give me modern context that many of the books I have on San Jose may be missing.

I continued south on Bascom, past the overpass of 280. Here, the Santa Clara Valley Medical Center marks Fruitdale to the west. It’s a campus of large and imposing buildings. The east side of the street features San Jose City College,

Beside me flies another music store, Rasputin Records. Every corner sings around here. Now I enter San Jose. The road is wide and the asphalt is old and torn in places. Chipped paint completes the road like moss. The turn lane is hard to find, and I do my best to stay on the right side. I swerve into the parking lot for Streetlight and park like an asshole. I contemplate keeping it that way, but my conscience is convinced that I’m a good person today. And so I re-park.

Take two. I ease into the parking bay with an exacting precision. Bascom Avenue isn’t exactly teeming with life, but there’s lots of cars huffing and puffing down it. It’s sunny out now so I get it. Few people walk on the sidewalks. I get out and look up and down the wide street searching for traces of my friend or his car. Eventually, he makes it and we walk into the land of Streetlight Records.

It’s a vast store which invites you to get lost inside. Vinyl records, video game CDs, DVD players, Blurays, laserdiscs, lost media, music posters, trinkets, and a whole lot more vinyl records. I glide past the rock and the pop and the folk and the jazz and the soul. Every artist you’ve ever heard of is listed, plus more you’ve never heard of. I give myself a small budget, and I decide to buy something for my friend. I find music I love, and music I’ve always wanted to get into. I buy my friend a record of Magdalena Bay’s, and he gets me Hayley William’s newest CD. I’m looking through discographies and discussions of Magdalena Bay on my phone when I see an ad for an upcoming show. They’re the supporting act to Hayley Williams at Shoreline Amphitheatre a week from now. What are the odds?!? My friend says it’s a sign that we must go.

The staff at Streetlights is all, unsurprisingly, very very knowledgeable on music. They make recommendations and tell me where I could find specific artists I want. Most CDs are less than $5, some of the packaged and more popular ones still only hovering around $10-12. I see signs for events coming up, and as it turns out, Streetlight Records does listening parties on Thursdays and Fridays for most major releases. You can get first access to vinyls, CDs, merchandise, and even free goodies at them. It’s a great way to give a new album a listen with fellow fans of that genre or artist.

We continue to walk around the shop, finding music of genres that I was hardly aware of. As I left with my choices for the day, that I could own and share for good, I found myself already planning my next visits.

Now, I’m listening to one of the new CDs I picked up in the car. I drive past a host of strip malls on Bascom going south. Little markets and tae kwon do classes and barbershops and law offices and tattoo parlors and video stores. If you continue south, you’d end up all the way in Campbell. But as I get closer and closer, I keep my eyes peeled for food instead.

Instead of food, I see something else that piques my interest. I pull over and stop beside it. I pick up a tea at a Thai restaurant to occupy myself as my mind ponders this sign next door. The words entice me in. They pull me in like the record I was listening to. It’s a psychic office, and two hands on top of an ethereal sky beckon me. The hands say, “ YOUR FUTURE IS IN YOUR HANDS ”. The sign went on below, about making hopes and dreams a reality. My friend and I had just seen a sign for the future with the show we would go to. What other signs in my life could I make reality? What could I talk to a psychic about?

As I peeled off of Bascom onto the highway, I heard Beck’s faint tones through my speaker. “Don't talk to me if you're looking for somebody to cry on”. I have no clue what I’m looking for exactly but I don’t think it is that. What if I’m looking for somebody to rely on instead?

I maintain that strip malls are the premier social hubs of the country. They’re the most happening parts of any town in a sense. The full tapestry. People go there whether they’re up or down. Sometimes you just have to go to a store or a business to get some shit done no matter the size of the pit in your soul. I often write about experiences around the south bay that could be considered special days or weekend outings, but most of our days and lives aren’t like that. Most days, we don’t do something new or go to a special bar or discover a local subculture or find a new park. Most days, we go to work and school and then go home. Maybe a strip mall if it’s a special day. The strip mall is where it all goes down. From Bascom to Story to El Camino.

The rains have turned one side of the parking lot into a lake. The drain inlet is completely blocked by fall leaves. Luckily, the rains have subsided for now. A man wearing a hoodie to hide himself grunts his way past me into the supermarket. He’s wet and damp but he’s not concerned. In the other corner of the lot, a fidgeting man sits alone in his car as he scrolls. Not a care for the world as the cars entering the lot behind him slalom around his car and curse him. There’s lots of yellers. A woman hands off a parcel of food to the man in the car, and he shoots me a look as he takes it. As if to say to me, I see you watching.

I do see him. And I do hear the cursing of the cars in the lot. And the water rushing in the trenches. My senses are always elevated after I run. I hear a ruckus at the corner of the L-shaped strip mall. Unbeknownst to me, Blue Max cocktails is having a rocking Friday karaoke night. I walk past, wishing I had my ID or a normal set of clothes on. I ask one of the yellers how late the place is open, in case I think of coming back tonight. He takes a hit (or whatever one does with a cigarette) and says he’s been smoking since ‘68. Within minutes he’s opening up to me about his conspiracies about the Sumerians and humans being aliens and the overarching world cabal. All interspersed with normal conversation about SoCal of course.

That may have seemed like a crazy leap, but I find this happens often. If you talk to people a little and enjoy doing it enough, they quickly open up and share their personal beliefs and world framings. I do it too, and maybe that enables others to mirror me. Conspiracy stoner wackos can be easily dismissed, but what I find is that it’s relatively simple to meet normal people that have “fringe” or “out there” or “kooky” worldviews. I use my quotations there intentionally.

Since seeing the sign for the psychic on Bascom, I had been noticing the conflicting worldviews of the world around me in constant motion everywhere I looked. The Baader-Meinhof phenomenon in action. I noticed it in my friends, and I noticed it in my surroundings. A freemasonry lodge near work. A church of Scientology near home. A Reiki expert by the bar. Religious temples and churches in corners I had never observed. None of this is science, this is something entirely else. I believe in science of course, I study it and live my life by it. Carl Sagan wrote it best, “How can we even make intelligent decisions in our own lives - if we don’t grasp the underlying [science]”. He was critical of pseudosciences due to their ability to mislead and misinform people while selling them something short of reality. Perhaps the pseudosciences are more enticing to some due to their lack of access to real and interesting science. Yet, regardless of what us outsiders feel and think, the idea of self help through a pseudoscience based belief system or alternative community is an attractive and popular one.

I’ve also personally always doubted therapy. Not for any rational reason, but I’ve always felt like I could point out my flaws better than any other person could. A friend says that thinking therapy aims to achieve that alone probably means that I need therapy. On the other hand, organized religion also doesn’t interest me much. Cults interest me, but that’s where that should and does end I think.

What is left though is the world of self-determined spirituality. That world, especially online and in California, is dominated by a New Age spirituality that is cobbled together from a mishmash of sources including Japanese traditions, Native American worldviews, various Hindu philosophies, Buddhist temperaments, and so on - all adorned with a hint of showmanship. Psychics and general practitioners/healers of New Age wisdom have grown increasingly popular, even more than in the 60s and 70s, which was meant to be their heyday in this part of the country. But spiritual gurus have never been limited to solely places like the Haight-Ashbury or the Sierra Madre Canyon, regardless of what my first instinct is.

The sight of the psychic sign on Bascom continued bouncing around in my head for months on end. All these other thoughts did too. Until, eventually I just called and decided to meet the psychic. Not for my sake, of course not. I just want to support a local business on Bascom, and maybe learn something about myself while doing that. That’s all it was, I said to myself.

On another moody day, I park beside a small building, with one of my wheels poking out into the sidewalk. An unfound sense of preservation and self-assertiveness had hit me. I am a good person and good things will happen to me! Why does it feel like an audition? I felt like I was coming up on something but the nausea was replaced by an intense want to be liked and understood. I’d rather be coming up on something.

It’s a small room which houses the Campbell Psychic here. Claudia Camden is her name. This is one of a few offices. I think about shooting her a message, but there she appears on cue. A maroon sweater and maroon nails. The colors are very well coordinated.

“You need to repark. Your wheels are on the sidewalk.”

I laugh it off. Then it strikes me that she’s telling me and not asking me. I don’t know why my urge was to laugh it off like she’s a friend of mine. I park again, and I wonder how much of my life has been wasted re-parking my car instead of preparing for my future.

The future was what the next half hour was about. I had some questions prepared, but we meandered between things of the past and predictions of the future. I got into it much more than I thought I would. I tried to really open myself up to her and the supposed energies in the room, and I was making progress in my thoughts. I don’t want to share personal details, but getting a reading on the way I am and what’s to come was oddly very reaffirming. I hardly knew half the terminology or what the cards meant, but she patiently explained them in plain terms that made sense to me. I don’t think she was using generic terms for herself, but rather for others as terms they may recognize. She had no qualms expanding on them further. She made harrowingly accurate and intuitive predictions about the way I am and what’s been happening in my life. She made predictions for my future given it all. She stressed one thing repeatedly though. Do the things I recommend, they could help. But the specific actions don’t matter. You don’t have to burn sage. Just do whatever it is you do deliberately and with intentionality. That’s what lets you own your future best. That’s the only thing I need to own.

She was spectacular.

Cynically, I could say that this was all nothing more than guesswork and advanced deduction by her. But something must and something needs to explain how so many of her calls and readings made sense in the context of my life and situations. At the very least, something must explain why I felt hugely better after the reading. We don’t have to reject science in order to see utility in something like this. Discard the things that mean little, carry the bits that do.

I sat in my now correctly parked car spiritually motivated. I looked out towards the Valley Fair parking lot with an idea of what’s to come. A tumultuous 10 weeks supposedly. I drive to the creek to mull it over, and deliberately ignore the GPS on my phone. I drive down Steven’s Creek, firm in my actions. It reminds me of Bascom now, as I see the road in a new light of life even though I’ve seen and been down it a million times. The music in my car matches my food, and it’s slow and folky. It’s nice to slow down. Maybe the only thing I take away from all this is the act of being deliberate, but that’s more than enough. I suspect I’ll be back for more help in some months time, though.

The more I read and the more I speak with people I internalize something. There are two ways to view all things. And the first way is always easier to do. We can demean someone else’s spirituality. We can bemoan someone’s choice of community. We can handwave someone’s favorite part of town. We could talk Bascom Avenue away as some boring arterial street with far too many abandoned buildings and old shops. “There’s nothing to do in San Jose!” Or we can choose to view it as something else. As a living road with a cast of revolving doors waiting to be discovered. A portal to places worth going to over and over again. A place worth revitalizing. A community where you could feel better and learn something about yourself. I choose to do the latter. Because everything is more than meets the eye.

A few things to end on. There’s been some major Quakes news in the past week. For one, the season has ended and the Earthquakes, despite losing the final game of the season, qualified to the playoffs as #4 in the western conference. This means San Jose will host our first ever playoff game at PayPal Park - the last time we had a home playoff game we were still at BuckShaw Stadium in 2012. So tonight, on November 24 at 7 PM, we host Houston in the first game in the first round of playoffs. The second game is away, and if a third is necessary to split the teams, San Jose has earned the right to host the deciding final game. It’s all very exciting stuff on the field, but it’s the off-field stuff that’s captured the news lately.

Last year, John Fisher announced that the San Jose Earthquakes were up for sale to the delight of a majority of Quakes fans. After he had bungled up the situations with his owned teams in Oakland so majorly, it’s no surprise. After a year of being up for sale, last week, the San Jose Earthquakes were sold to the Global Capital founder Heath Freeman for $625 million. Most Quakes fans have celebrated getting rid of John Fisher from what I’ve seen. A sign saying FJF (standing for Fuck John Fisher) or a comment saying FJF has been present at games and in every comment section for years now. But I can’t say I’m completely excited for this new owner either, who is the owner of a private equity company. I guess it’s better than being bought out by private equity outright, but who’s to say. I’ve been reading rumors by MLS journalists that the new owner would want to drum up more interest in the team by extending games and events to other parts of the bay, specifically San Francisco. We’re a San Jose team. I hope we don’t pander so hard to other parts of the bay, SF already gets most events and sports teams. I guess the south bay has the Niners now, but they are still firmly a San Francisco team. Overall though, I am cautiously optimistic about the new owner for the time being. As long as we stay in San Jose and can make some bigger signings in the future, I think I’d be happier. And I think you should be excited regardless. Change is fun, right?

I suspect that I enjoy this. Anyways, stay safe in the rains and storms of El Niño!

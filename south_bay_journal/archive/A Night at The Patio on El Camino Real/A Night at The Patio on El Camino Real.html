A Night at The Patio on El Camino Real

A bit of nostalgia through a slightly grown lens

Originally posted on substack on August 30, 2026

“What can I say about life? Do I praise it for letting you live or damn it for allowing the rest?

It’s been an odd year. Part of it has been watching friends move out of the area. Some found work elsewhere, some headed to more school elsewhere, and some moved with their partners or families altogether. Life changes, and people scatter. I guess we can expect this.

That’s why I was gladly surprised to hear when a friend of mine moved… and it was still near and within the area, in the south bay. Some five miles away from her family and home into Mountain View. You know, it felt nice to keep a friend around.

The first day I was at the apartment, everyone was idly watching music videos together in celebration. Some 2000s smash hits, some deeper cuts, some new singles from the past weeks. Some Fun. songs came on, and I couldn’t help but feel this tight feeling that I always do with their music. How incredibly gut wrenching I find some of the melodies and lyrics. They’re nostalgic and I think I’ve just tied too much meaning as a kid to what are probably just throwaway lyrics. I looked around and beyond my friends I saw this apartment, and my chest panged again. The apartment reminded me of where I grew up in Sunnyvale. Everything was converging towards this odd feeling of painful nostalgia which music does a real great job in bringing out. I cannot help but feel that feeling to blinding degrees. Nowadays, even decisions for my future are made with the thought of how I’ll feel looking back at them in the future that’s even further along.

What is this feeling? Maybe you’ve felt it too. Maybe you can’t help but see the past in everything you point your eyes towards. That feeling should be confronted.

So in another attempt to answer the question that no human has ever had an answer to, I took a trip down to my past. Hoping to find the seeds of my future. And unravel what it is that I want. So to Sunnyvale I went.

The King’s Road is a common street name in the towns of England. The monarchy bleeds through every aspect of life there. Here we’re not so far from royalty either, but it tends to be Spanish.

El Camino Real is the royal road connecting California. It’s the road of the kings built by the Spanish missionaries of the past. The original route through which you could travel between all 21 Missions, at least that was the intention. Kind of like a Spanish Silk Road where religion was traded instead of spice, at least that’s how it was sold to me in school and how I subsequently bought it. I did a project about Mission San Fernando Rey, and as great of a job I thought I had done, I don’t think my class really touched upon any too many of the less pleasant aspects of the treatment of native Americans in the diorama or ‘essay’. I was so naive, I wasn’t sure if the idea of “spreading Christianity” was meant to be seen as a charitable act akin to my teacher sharing pizza with the class, or comparable to the supposed lonely men who spread disease and drug through Halloween chocolate. I just don’t know if I believed in malice back then.

But I did believe in El Camino Real. I mean, I lived off of El Camino Real for a good while, so it would be weird if I didn’t believe in the street existing. I lived by the community center in Sunnyvale, where the In N Out on El Camino is.

The modern El Camino Real runs through the state from Sonoma county all the way to the Mexican border as various different designations. It’s Highway 101 at parts, Highway 1 at points, Interstate 280, small county roads, walking paths, and, through the bay, largely State Route 82.

Pull up your choice of map and you’ll see that section of El Camino Real running parallel to Interstate 280 and 101, sandwiched in the modern suburbia between. But don’t get it twisted. It is a far older road than the former two. You’ll see the mission bells on the side of the road whenever you’re on the “historic El Camino Real” blazed by the missionaries, because supposedly only those parts of the modern road align with its original historic path. The reality is that these bells don’t necessarily indicate any kind of authenticity or history.

In 1769, the first complete voyage up the coast of California was made by Spanish conquistador Gaspar de Portolá and his crew for the Kingdom of New Spain. They voyaged from the region around San Diego all the way up to the modern San Francisco Bay and on to Sonoma. And yes, that is the same Portola after whom Portola Valley in the bay area is named after. Obviously though, they were not placing mission bells everywhere on their path. What they did do was establish Alta California as a state north of the similarly named Baja California. Their voyage documented and opened up California to the rest of Spanish soldiers, settlers, and missionaries. As more native indigenous people were encountered, more Missions were set up, and the Spaniards continued to spread their empire through religion and enslavement. And when the Missions were set up, bells were placed on their sites for timekeeping and announcement purposes.

The mission bells you see on El Camino Real themselves have only existed and were originally placed in 1906 by CalTrans as a tourism and beautification project, exactly one mile apart. Since then up to 2012, the markers have been continually replaced and maintained by various organizations and agencies. I recommend reading this if you want to learn more, it gives a lot more context and now it’s fun trying to date when a marker must be from when you see the.

The markers stand on El Camino Real today, a reminder of the history of the area. El Camino Real is still a road on which thousands of lives are lived. But I wonder, which one of the paths that our generation forges will be remembered in the future? Which one of them should or shouldn’t?

There’s a world in which unions and revolutionaries and weekends never existed. Time off work and things like holidays never existed. I mean, time and measuring it was hardly a pressing matter until labor became a thing. It’s a scary world that many moneyed folk want to bring back, but thankfully it’s not real. So on a holiday Monday that I had off, I made all of our revolutionary predecessors proud: I took the day off work and decided to go get drunk. I took off to go hang out at El Camino Real, visit some peewee stomping grounds, and also to check out a bar near where I used to live that I had obviously never been since I was a child.

The Patio lies on East El Camino Real, barely a block down from Golfland. Now there’s a place that houses memories. Maybe I would swing by there before the Patio too. I took my time and left a little after noon from home. It was nearing two, and I parked in the small lot for the Patio. To either side of the bar, there were small Indian strip malls restaurants enticing me in. It’s always them that taste the best. I resisted, and walked into the bar, which had just opened at 2 PM.

It was dead. Not a single soul. I stepped out like I had never come in. Maybe I need to walk around a bit and let the crowd come in. Let it become the place I had heard and read it was, rather than a place that I would mischaracterize because I went too damn early on a weekday.

There’s this oddly huge median nearby, covered in dead grass and sparse trees. It does include one neat feature; there’s a mission bell signifying the historic El Camino Real. I wonder in which iteration of bells this one was added in. It’s likely it’s one of the newer ones, I think.

I crossed El Camino Real, whose intersection may be over a hundred feet wide.It’s always so odd to walk past a bunch of cars as the single pedestrian, while all the drivers look like they want nothing more than to blow right past you. I saw Golfland, and ran over there instead. I went past alone, wishing I was in there with friends whacking golf balls onto the street and paying for nine holes while doing all eighteen. I was alone though, so I trudged on El Camino.

The road is a commercial strip entirely. I walk past car repair shops, gas stations, hole in the wall food, massage centers, law firms with a Saul-ish quality to them, car dealerships of exorbitant prices, barber shops, grocery stores, a Michael’s, a sushi spot, a liquor store, a vacant furniture store. I have no one to visit the furniture store with, or no reason to. I’d like to though.

Eventually I get to the In-N-Out on Remington. My dentist’s office is right by it too. I need to floss more, I remember. Before I can continue worrying about the health of my teeth though, I see someone with a shake. So I buy one of those, the sugar will help keep my teeth happy. Problem solved. The first time I had ever been to this In-N-Out, it was in elementary school with my principal and a random other kid. Looking back, I’m not sure why a trip to a burger spot with school faculty was a good idea for a vegetarian child, but I’m glad it did happen in a way. Because it let me connect where my home was to the bustling life on the street of El Camino, just a block down. I wandered past one of the apartment buildings my family used to live in, my shake in hand. I walked another block, and walked past another apartment building that we lived in. I wasn’t getting anything profound from this. I thought I might. The only thing I noticed was that the grass mound in the center of the apartment complex seemed tiny now. It really used to seem like a hill.

Whatever, I wasn’t sure what I was even looking for. Some people in the apartment complex walked by, probably confused why there was someone walking around like a tourist in an exhibit, In-N-Out shake and all.

The Sunnyvale Community Center is very close by too, the other side of Remington from where I was. I decided to walk through, soak in the lake and the people and all the memories.

But my memories had been altered. Or rather the community center grounds had been altered. I had seen things under construction when I absent mindedly drove by a couple of times visiting the dentist. But I had no clue at the extent of the renovations . The lake was much smaller now, a kind of arced semi circle instead of the infinity or 8 like shape from the past. One side of the lake was now dedicated to a playground for kids, and it was packed. So many kids buzzing and bees playing. Even the fountain was altered.

I took a lap of the lake, which was shorter than it used to be. It was still nice though. I struggled to connect any of my memories of this lake with the disgusting algae and duck based lake I had seen as a kid. This was clearly nicer though. Maybe swans would visit along with ducks now.

That was enough of the community center. The only community I needed was the bar. I spent the next ten minutes researching how to floss my teeth, while opting for the residential streets back to the Patio. El Camino Real was too loud, and I was feeling far too earnest and rational now to make any more musings about that heavily trafficked road. I need to stop thinking about the past.

Back at the Patio, it was raucous. Well, I could hear faint music from outside, so clearly it had gotten rolling. It was almost 4:30 now, a perfect time to get in.

Temper your expectations. This isn’t the kind of place featured in worldly magazines. It doesn’t boast a ‘buzzy atmosphere’. It’s not matcha inspired beer or special Colombian IPA pour or something. There wasn’t a blindingly bright QR-code based menu screen above. No, it was just a bar. A simple bar on the right side, with some small black tables along the left side. The smell of alcohol and gas in the air. That might be my nose still picking up something from El Camino, to be fair. In the back, a small hallway that was hard to see past or in. What if it opened up to another realm? Or maybe, it was a portal to the Patio in Palo Alto.

Oh yeah, there’s another bar called the Patio in Palo Alto. It’s in downtown there, and it’s a more upscale spot. The kind of place that you could take a date if you wanted to impress them with your lack of reaction at the $22 appetizers. You then saunter past a gaggle of badge wearing folk, none of whom are dancing. Your jig past them makes you seem as the reincarnation of …well someone who can actually dance. Your date’s impressed, and your bank account is down about a hundred dollars before you know it.

Sometimes, the Patio in Palo Alto shifts from being a bar with some food to a part-time club with a vibey outdoor section. It’s like a spacier Molly’s. Cool place, can be a cool time; don’t get me wrong. But not the kind of place you always remember. It gets lost in your mind with another ten places that are about the same. The conversations or the drinks don’t feel as real or raw or genuine as you’d like. Even Molly’s, a bar I frequent too often, isn’t really a dive bar like that . I realized I described it as so in my writing about it on this substack, but that was simply another era. In the past three years, I have become familiar with so many types of watering holes. The US lacks a nationwide bar chain, but imagine there was one. Some bars immediately spring to your mind as being part of this imaginary chain. Others would never meet the requirements of being a franchise or part of the chain, but that’s why we love them, I guess. Which one do you prefer?

St likely, there was no portal to the Patio in Palo Alto. I took inventory of the room and bar in front of me instead. It was pretty dead. There’s a darts board by the other wall. No one playing. To be fair, it was still not that late. I’m gracious in making early judgements to any establishment I enter though, like a regal swan.

I stare and sit at the bar. It’s a bar and the bartender’s not there. Didn’t mind, it gave me a second to actually think about what to drink instead of just getting whatever came to my mind when asked. Eventually, the bartender swung by and I ordered a Pacifico. It was just what came to mind when I was asked.

I take a big swig. It feels good. I was thirsty, I was aimless, I slot right in here. I glance around to inspect the clientele of the day. Around me there’s a couple (?) in bickeringly slow conversation. I feel like they don’t want a third, in conversation or otherwise. I have nothing to do or say, so I finish the beer.

Okay, time to check out that room in the back. Is it indeed a dimension to another portal where I figure out why I spent hours walking through apartment buildings? A second drink and time to head back there. There’s no portal.

The backroom features black tables on the wall along with a pool table in the center. A Pabst Blue Ribbon light looms over the pool table. It feels bigger than it is, almost feels like it opens up outdoors even though it doesn’t. There’s a trippy rug hanging on the side wall as well. It glows in the dark, and I like the touch of psychedelic energy it gives the area. Some paintings, blow up palm trees, and a flag round up the room nicely. There’s a man drinking a PBR and shooting shots on the table alone. So we break bread and bump beers to begin talking.

The man I had met was from Alameda. He was in the Sunnyvale area for work every so often, and he would come to the Patio when he was here. Interesting was how he started, telling me about his various travels abroad. It was just him and I, so I proposed a game of pool.

The game began and all was well. But slowly he began to say things. And then he got drunker and he began to really say things. Within the course of our conversation he revealed himself to be a raging racist. I don’t know how he felt comfortable saying these things, and I don’t know why I had let this man feel this way. But I had already agreed to a pool game, and I was going to have to play it out. And now I felt like I had to win.

It started off alright, but slowly he began to sink them in. I was getting really nervous I was going to lose this incredibly important pool game to the Bay’s Most Racist Man (TM). I excused myself to the front of the bar, took a shot of vodka, and came back hoping to be fired up.

Nope, I lost. Probably shouldn’t have been drinking so much. Honestly, I have no tact or skill or plan when playing pool. I let people know that I’m terrible, but I say this to people as a defense mechanism. I would like to be good. I like to grab the chalk and put it on my cue stick, as if I know what that does other than vaguely let me hit the ball nicer. Mr Racist is leaving now, he has an early work morning. I hope he’s late to work. El Camino Real gets a lot of traffic in the morning, maybe the road will cooperate with me and make it happen. A crash or two. How low I have stooped. I guess he stooped low first.

I had been standing in the back room alone for a while now. There’s crates and crates of liquor and beer here. They’re warm but I’m still tempted to pick one up since no one would notice.

Integrity, integrity, the right thing when no one watches. That’s what I was meant to learn in elementary school. That’s why my principal took me to In-N-Out, to become a fine adult. I didn’t steal a beer. A group of four people came in just then, as if to let me know that I would’ve been caught anyways.

It’s two couples. Another older lady followed, who seemed to run the bar in some capacity. They were all regulars though from the way the conversation went. Sensing someone there for the first time, one of the guys started talking to me. A man with a wispy beard and dark blue baseball hat. His name was Mike, and not only was he a regular but also a member of the Patio’s tournament winning pool team. More importantly, he was definitely not racist. His wife and I talked, and they asked if I wanted a game of pool.

Mike was good at pool. Clearly, I mean he played most days and had won local tournaments. Then, he pulled out his custom black billiards case and I realized he was probably more than just good at pool. I watched him play the other guy, either named Arty or RD. I was only hearing all these names. They were both awfully nice to me.

Now, RD was not only a looker but also good at billiards. And yet, I watched Mike clear the set of solids with ease. The game ended, and Mike turned to me. He asked me to put some in. I can’t remember if that’s what he said or if I was still thinking of Golfland. I showed him my stance.

So apparently, I have to completely rethink my pool stance and technique. He told me how to stand, how to aim, where you want your feet, how to plan for the next shot, and so on and so on. Then, he told me the most important bit.

“You should be using no muscle and no wrist. It’s a pendulum. Your arm moves back and forth, that’s it. A pendulum.”

A pendulum you say. No power needed. Nice to hear, especially since I lack muscle. I start mimicking the motion back at him. I’m doing it, he’s doing it. It was glorious, just two men doing incredibly slow robot impressions to each other. RD is cheers-ing me on, I feel great. I started hitting some balls too, and lo and behold, I was making contact. I was making solid contact, even getting precise! Why didn’t I meet Mike before I met the slur-happy fuck from before? I felt great and I was even ready for a game against Mike. Straight into the deep end, I was ready to play card jitsu with the sensei.

I lost, pretty quickly. Mike didn’t take it easy, no not at all. He had a reputation at the Patio. I was kind of getting the hang of the motion, but my pendulum was still too stiff and jerky. Not like Mike’s. That was my retirement of pool for the night, I would rather just drink and watch Mike and friends play, glean some strategy.

My thirst returned so I stumbled through the people in the back to get back to the bar. It had gotten way more crowded in the back since I had begun my foolish-ruleless-pool-with-one-tool-and-one-human-school shenanigan. The black circular tables were filled and the Patio was bumping. I meandered through a few conversations, including a guy who was sad to find out he was no longer the youngest person in the bar. Well I don’t feel that way. The lake from when I was a kid is already half of what it was. Literally.

There are some pinball machines against the wall on the side too. One is Indiana Jones themed, and as a man with a penchant for Hollywood po-sciencey anthropology and brown useless watches, I love Indiana Jones. I don’t have any quarters though, so I can only look at it. And intermittently watch the back of the bar come to life, at 8PM on a Monday night.

The Patio in Sunnyvale really is a stark difference to the Patio in Palo Alto. At the latter you may meet a couple interesting people, interspersed with the same three conversations about the industry . At the Patio, it seemed as if everyone in there had a life and day and story of their own, but it was something only they were fit to tell. Some stories are less enjoyable or less important or more racist than others, but that doesn’t determine which ones are told. Hell, I’m still writing about mission bells in this post. Maybe I need to wisen up and write about Ohlone jewelry instead.

I went back to the front of the bar now. It was quieter than the back. The couple from before, a new but old lady, and the bartender. Everyone’s watching television. There’s a special on Coors for whatever reason, so I get a can of it even though I don’t love the beer. El dinero primero right? It washes my two pool losses down well.

I’m watching a movie that the lady next to me says is a classic. I’ve never seen it. I’m telling her stories of also growing up nearby, but I watched more Bollywood. I listened to Feliz Navidad on Christmas instead. Wait, what the fuck am I talking about? As if she cares? I’m very drunk, I really need some food. Behind me there’s a vending machine, which would have to suffice before I get an actual meal.

The scummy thing you could do with some old vending machines was just buy stuff until it fucked up sometimes. Eventually it would spit out way more change than it was meant to. For every few dollars you spent, you could get a couple extra in change through some dollar coin change. Way better than all those stupid gambling sites these days.

I tried the vending machine behind the bar next to the bathroom. I didn’t expect any extra money; it looked a hair nicer and newer than the vending machines at school. Technology, baby. It whirred, as loud as the TV nearby. Everyone was listening in. Or maybe it just felt that way. The whirring stopped. The bag of cheddar ruffles stayed still, on the edge, but not falling. Just my luck. No ruffles for me. And I would really like some ruffles. I would like so much more in my life. The prospect of turning around and having to face the crowd and face the music of their silence hit me. I was surely succumbed to having just another beer.

But… miracles do happen, and sometimes you do get the pendulum right. Some life entered the vending machine, and the bag of chips tipped slightly. The left side of the bag leaned and fell first, then all of it came down. It crashed on the bed of the vending machine with a probably impossibly noticeable sound, but I imagined the three people behind me at the bar turning their heads around in shock. I doubt they were. I tell you, I grabbed that bag like I stole it. I fucking love machines that work. And I hate that I know I’ll be back at the Patio sometime. Looking for my future in a slightly derelict vending machine. And maybe then, I’ll have a reason to celebrate for myself.

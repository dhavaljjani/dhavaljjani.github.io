The South Bay takes on the World Cup

Plus, the Olympics coming soon, a hiking challenge idea, and a short retelling of the second pub golf that happened

Originally posted on substack on September 12, 2026

In case you missed it, the World Cup happened this summer. One of the biggest celebrations of sporting unity in the world. Alongside a celebration of the world’s most brazenly corrupt organizations and figures. Hand in hand.

The San Francisco Bay Area hosted 6 games at the World Cup, including one Round of 32 game. And of course, when we say “San Francisco Bay Area”… we refer instead to Santa Clara where Levis’s Stadium actually is. I say let’s just get on with it and rename it to the Santa Clara Bay Area entirely. The Niners play here, and every other tech company from the city comes down to Santa Clara when it IPOs and decides to destroy the world or whatever anyways. Shrug.

I kid. But the fact that the games themselves were held in the south bay instead of the city was a big deal. And I think San Jose (and the rest of the south bay) benefitted from this World Cup more than San Francisco might have. In San Jose specifically, the Earthquakes partnered with the downtown association to put out big screens to watch all 104 games at San Pedro Square. A seismic success, I think. It was undoubtedly the place to be throughout the World Cup, especially if you didn’t have tickets. And who did? I love soccer, but I’m not shelling out $400 for any single-day thing. Michael Jackson could rise from the dead and do a show in my bathtub, and I wouldn’t go if it cost that much. I’d just stop showering or something. Anyways, the World Cup at San Pedro Square:

This was opening day of the group stages, when South Korea defeated Czechia 2-1. One of the things I loved seeing and found really interesting was using the games of the World Cup to sleuth out the crowd’s preferences. No surprises, San Jose went hard in cheering for and supporting South Korea. Alongside Mexico, the USA, and numerous Latin countries of course.

Generally, this area has a pretty long history with soccer, specifically international soccer. The last time the US hosted the World Cup in 1994, the Bay Area hosted our games at Stanford Stadium. Brazil famously played here on their journey to winning the World Cup, and stayed in Los Gatos. Here’s a great read about that , the first time the US took on the sport by selling out stadiums and packing them for USMNT and Brazil.

Aside from the on the pitch soccer, the World Cup was such a great time this summer just because of all the spontaneous hangouts it led to. Meeting up with friends and family to watch games at someone’s backyard or their apartment. Go to a new bar or make a new friend at the shop as you watch the last ten minutes of an intense knockout game. Everyone at work talking about the results of the game the day before and trying their best to pretend like they don’t care so much about getting their bracket right. It’s just such a good time, and I had such a great time this summer here in San Jose despite the fact that the two countries I chose to support both went out relatively early.

In 2028, the Olympics are also coming stateside to Los Angeles. Interestingly, for the women’s soccer tournament in the Olympics, multiple games (including a Round of 16 match) are going to be hosted in San Jose at PayPal Park! I’m so incredibly excited for this, and I hope the USWNT can play in San Jose on their journey to winning another Olympic games. Rose Lavelle, American midfielder, was just nominated for the Ballon d’Or , and the rest of the women’s team is primed for a deep medal-winning run! So, if you caught the soccer bug this summer, I encourage you to try and pick up tickets for the San Jose Women’s Soccer games in the general sale for LA28. I know I’m going to try to. The Olympics in San Jose?? That’s ridiculous and awesome.

I do hope the World Cup and upcoming Olympics lead to more excitement for the women’s and men’s game here in San Jose. The Quakes and Bay FC exist, but sometimes it’s easy to forget about them due to lack of coverage. I mean, I was at SJC some months ago and take a look at this:

I think it’s a little crazy that you can be at SJC inside the Hudson’s shop looking through the local sports team corner…and not find a single Quakes or Bay FC shirt or hat. They got stuff for the SF Giants, for the Golden State Warriors, for the San Jose Sharks, for the 49ers. But not a single San Jose soccer thing, even as there sits some outrageously expensive and hideous generic FIFA World Cup merch. I guess when you’re a “host city” you gain access to wondrous things such as a plushy eagle with a FIFA logo on his forehead for $39.99. Not a mention of the local teams even though the stadium is just across the airport. You can literally see it at any gate out the window!

If there’s a benefit the World Cup could have had to soccer in this country, it’s that we all try to collectively love each other a little more across the globe. And if not that, we should all know who Krazy George is in San Jose. That should be required here!

The 8 Peaks of the Bay Area

Mountain or vista points are probably my favorite kinds of hikes to do. You get the satisfaction from knowing you endured some hardship and incline, your muscles feel the right kind of sore, and you get rewarded with an expansive view at the climax. The hike down flies by because of the natural high you’re still riding.

In the middle of this past summer, I finally hiked and summited Mt Diablo with a friend, something I’ve wanted to do for the longest time. Luckily I didn’t run into any tarantulas which are supposedly very prevalent in summer. And thankfully, I did get treated to clear skies and expansive views when I finally made it to the peak from Mitchell Canyon. From the peak, you could see the skyline of San Francisco, the plains and suburbs of Brentwood, the floodplains of the Sacramento River delta, and the many bridges of the bay like the Bay Bridge, Carquinez Bridge, and a faint Golden Gate Bridge. You could even see all the other high points in the area, such as Mt Tam and Mt Hamilton which could be spotted very prominently.

The question it brought to the forefront of my mind was, what are the defining mountains of the Bay Area? And, if you’re a peak-bagger, what would be the summits you have to hit? The ones that are worth it for not just the hike only, but the views, the flora and fauna in the area, the surrounding region, the history, and just the enjoyability of the hike up. So, I made the following map poster to hang up on the wall as I try to complete it.

Here’s my thought process, going clockwise around the bay:

  1. Mt Diablo makes it with ease. It’s arguably the most iconic bay area mountain and if you’re anywhere in the bay with a view, chances are you can see it looming in the distance. Gorgeous peak, gorgeous park with lots of history. The summit was a benchmark for surveying for much of California’s early history, and as someone who works with survey monuments occasionally, this adds a huge cool factor to the mountain.

  2. Mission Peak is the classic and most stereotypical south bay hike. Everyone hikes Mission Peak. If you have friends or family visiting you hike Mission Peak. If you want to get a group of people together with no idea of what to do, you hike Mission Peak. If you want to see some cows and maybe some horses, you go to Mission Peak. If you’re feeling down and out, take an aspirin and hike Mission Peak. On a clear day, you can see San Francisco from the peak, with the skyline glistening.

  3. This is an interesting one. Mt Hamilton can be driven up to by road on Highway 131 (a continuation of Alum Rock Blvd). Due to its height, it occasionally snows up by the summit like a few of the others on this list. Lick’s Observatory is also located at the summit, which is an astronomical observatory opened by the UC open to the public for tour and star-gazing on occasion. Nearby Mt Hamilton lies the higher Copernicus Peak, which is actually the highest point in Santa Clara County. By that regard, it’s technically the highest point in the classic nine county definition of the “bay area” entirely. But, Copernicus Peak summit is located completely on private property and cannot be accessed.

  4. My personal bias comes in here. I have good memories of getting up to Black Mountain a few times and I think it’s a must hike for one reason: great expansive views of the Santa Clara valley and the northern part of the south bay. The skyline ridge trail generally has great east-facing views, and I chose Black Mountain in particular due to it being an easier climb when compared to Castle Rock, Mt Bielawski, or other high points in the area.

  5. If you’ve ever wondered what that large cinder-block-looking-thing south of Almaden/Los Gatos is - that would be Mt Umunhum. The Cube, as it’s called, is a radar tower from the era of the Cold War, located at the summit. The view at the summit looks towards Los Gatos, Cupertino, Campbell, and South San Jose. This is one I definitely need to make it to, if only to properly try and grasp the size of the radar tower cube building.

  6. This is one I whacked onto the list because I wanted to include a mountain close to or in the city of San Francisco. San Bruno Mountain is outside the city limits but directly south, and is taller than any of the 43 hills of the city. The hike up to the ridgeline and summit does not look too strenuous, and photos online indicate expansive views of SF, Oakland, Alameda, and the bay itself.

  7. Mt Tam is constantly the most recommended spot anytime I talk to any hiker, no matter their experience level. Located just north of San Francisco in Marin County, the peak boasts views of the city, the bay, and the vast Pacific Ocean to the west. Supposedly, early morning ascents are the most magical because as you rise you get to poke out above the morning tule fog and experience something other-worldly.

  8. This is the only mountain I had never even heard of before when trying to make this list. Well over 4000 feet, it rounds out this list well by getting you out somewhere further but still staying in Sonoma County in the Bay Area. This mountain is actually composed of volcanic rock, and it receives the most snowfall out of any peak listed. Pictures of its green flanks are gorgeous and I can’t wait to make it out here. I’m also glad it exists for the sake of geographic variety and spacing on the map!

Here are some honorable mentions from other photos I see online and what else I read. There’s Mt Day, Loma Prieta , Montara Peak, Junipero Serra Peak, Mt Madonna, Mt Sizer, and so many more . We’re so lucky to have all these wonderful mountains so close by to discover. Feel free to try and complete this summit challenge before I ever do, or let me know if you already have.

The Campbell Pub Golf

The last thing I want to do end this post with is the second pub golf, which we did in Campbell. People really enjoyed the first one, so I made a new map and hung up more flyers in Mountain View and Campbell. The idea of vampires in July was hilarious to me when someone suggested it, and so that was the theme. The Campbell Bar Crawl Vampire Brawl! Here’s the course:

Overall the day went spectacularly, maybe better than the first. A lot of faces from the last pub golf, and a couple new ones who found the flyer or my reddit post. We had even better costumes this time in my opinion too, lots of vampires along with adjacent gothic outfits. I think I was a pretty damn good vampire too, and the bloody Marys only got me deeper in character. The trivia questions I came up with this time also stuck to the theme much better.

We ended up skipping The Vesper and Khartoum on the day because of timings and what the crowd was feeling, so we ended the crawl at 7 Stars Bar & Grill instead. And boy was that a great decision - I had talked to the owner there beforehand and somehow he had organized the regulars into being dressed up as vampire hunters! The best surprise I’ve ever gotten, it was such a fun atmosphere in there and a great place to end it with for karaoke. The red team won as well, which thematically made sense, but also because their team featured someone who could just down ‘em. Another shoutout to Tessora’s Winebar, who loved meeting our vampiric group and posted us on their Instagram stories (along with 7 Stars) and asked for a copy of the course map too. I’ll definitely be back there, for both the Pinot and the company of the wonderful owners.

I had forgotten to post about this pub golf on this substack beforehand, but for the next one I will definitely remember to do so. It’s hard to judge what avenue works best for getting the word out there, aside from just constant social media posting. I like putting out physical flyers, because I feel as though it brings a certain kind of person out - the type of person who would stumble upon a ridiculous flyer and message the freak from it to attend a costume based pub golf. I love those weirdos.
